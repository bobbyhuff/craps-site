Two numbers on a blackjack placard, "BLACKJACK PAYS 3 TO 2" or "BLACKJACK PAYS 6 TO 5," look
like a rounding difference. They aren't. It's the single biggest rule variation on the table,
bigger than the deck count, bigger than soft 17, bigger than every basic-strategy decision
combined. Here's the actual cost.

## The payout, not the odds

A natural blackjack, an Ace and a ten-value card on the first two cards, pays extra because it
beats every hand except a dealer blackjack. On a $10 bet:

* **3:2 blackjack** pays **$15**.
* **6:5 blackjack** pays **$12**.

Same hand, same bet, same luck. The player is paid $3 less every single time a $10 wager
turns into a natural, just because of what's printed on the felt.

The cut scales exactly with the bet: 6:5 always pays you 20% less than 3:2 on a natural,
whatever you had riding on the hand.

## What it costs over a session

Blackjacks land on roughly 4.8% of hands (about 1 in 21) dealt from a standard shoe, so the
per-hand payout cut translates into a fixed increase in the house edge, independent of any
strategy decision: switching from 3:2 to 6:5 raises the house edge by roughly **1.4
percentage points**, depending on the rest of the table's rules. That 1.4% applies to every
dollar wagered, not just the hands that end in a blackjack, because it's baked into the
long-run expected value of every hand dealt.

| Total Action | Approx. Additional Expected Cost of 6:5 |
|---|---:|
| $1,000 | ~$14 |
| $5,000 | ~$70 |
| $10,000 | ~$140 |
| $25,000 | ~$350 |
| $100,000 | ~$1,400 |

These are long-run expected-value estimates, the average cost if you played that much action
an enormous number of times. Any single session can land above or below this figure purely
from variance. The number that doesn't move is the rate: 6:5 costs about 1.4% more of
everything wagered, session after session, win or lose.

## Why 1.4 points is a big deal

Playing perfect basic strategy against a fair 3:2, S17, 6-deck game like the one dealt here
already gets the house edge down to roughly 0.5%. See [0stakes' own basic strategy
chart](/strategy/blackjack/basic-strategy/) for the exact rule-by-rule numbers. A 6:5 payout
alone adds roughly 1.4 points on top of that, nearly tripling the total edge, more than every
common basic-strategy mistake a casual player makes across an entire session combined. You
can play every hand exactly right and still hand back three times as much simply by sitting
down at the wrong table.

## The takeaway

**The payout printed on the table can matter more than many of the strategy decisions players
obsess over.** Splitting 8s or standing on a hard 16 against a dealer 6 matters, but a single
glance at the placard before sitting down matters more. Prefer 3:2 blackjack and avoid 6:5
