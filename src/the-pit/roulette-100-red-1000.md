**Quick answer:** On an American roulette wheel, a $100 red bet wins $100 on 18 pockets and loses $100 on the other 20 (18 black plus 0 and 00). Across 1,000 independent spins, the **expected loss is $5,263.16**, but an actual 1,000-spin run can finish much better or worse.

## The experiment, without the hype

Assume an American wheel with 38 equally likely pockets, one fixed $100 bet on red for each of 1,000 spins, and enough money to place every bet. There is **no betting progression** and no stopping when the bankroll goes negative. This is a mathematical model, not a claim that any one person will experience precisely the average.

Each spin has an 18/38 = **47.37%** chance of a $100 profit and a 20/38 = **52.63%** chance of a $100 loss. Red and black are not 50/50 because both green pockets lose a red bet.

## What does the math say?

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Bet each spin | $100 |
| Spins | 1,000 |
| Total amount wagered | $100,000 |
| Expected red wins | 473.68 |
| Expected red losses | 526.32 |
| Expected net result | **−$5,263.16** |
| Standard deviation of net result | **about $3,157.89** |

The standard deviation measures the considerable spread around the average. It doesn't imply a guaranteed range. The exact net result after 1,000 spins is `$100 × (2 × number_of_red_wins − 1,000)`. To break even or finish ahead, red must land at least 500 times: a result above the expected 473.68 wins. The exact probability can be calculated with a binomial distribution, rather than inventing a simulated win rate.

## What if you switch from red to black?

Nothing about the expectation changes on a fair American wheel. Black also occupies 18 of 38 pockets. Switching colors after losses, alternating colors, and following streaks can't remove the two zero pockets.

## What if you quit after a win?

Quitting can change the amount wagered and the distribution of session outcomes, but it doesn't create positive expected value on each spin. A short winning session and a profitable long-term betting system are not the same thing.

## American vs European roulette

A single-zero European wheel has 37 pockets, so red wins 18/37 of the time. The expected loss for $100 per spin over 1,000 spins is **$2,702.70**, roughly half the American-wheel expected loss. Special rules such as *la partage* can change the expectation for certain even-money bets; they're not assumed here.

## FAQ

### Is betting red safer than betting a single number?

Red hits more often, but on an American wheel standard red and straight-up bets both carry approximately a 5.26% house edge. Their volatility is very different.

### Can you win after 1,000 spins?

Yes. Winning outcomes are possible despite the negative expectation. A particular sequence of spins is not guaranteed to match the average.

### Does red become more likely after ten black spins?

No. Assuming independent fair spins, the probability of red on the next spin remains 18/38.
