**Quick answer:** If you're new to craps, start with the **Pass Line**, learn when to add **Odds**, and consider **Place 6 and 8** if you want bets you can make while a point is already on. The lowest mathematical house edge doesn't guarantee the smallest dollar loss: bet size, total action, and session length still matter.

Craps is full of colorful betting systems. A good beginner strategy is simpler: choose bets with understandable rules and relatively low house edges, then make sure the amount you risk fits your play-money bankroll. Here's how five practical approaches compare, using the same standard payouts shown on the [0stakes craps table](/games/craps/).

## Quick ranking: bets before betting systems

| Beginner approach | House edge | Why consider it | Watch out for |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Don't Pass (12 pushes) | ~1.36% per resolved flat wager | Slightly lower edge than Pass Line | The come-out 12 is a push; some players prefer betting with the shooter |
| Pass Line | ~1.41% per resolved flat wager | Easy starting point and clear game flow | You must place it on the come-out |
| Place 6 or Place 8 | ~1.52% per resolved Place bet | Flexible, straightforward payouts | Risk stays exposed until the number hits, seven appears, or you take the bet down |
| Come | ~1.41% per resolved flat wager | Similar math to Pass Line after a point is on | More moving bets can be harder to track |
| Pass Line + Odds | ~1.41% on flat bet; **0% on Odds portion** | Reduces blended edge *as a percentage of total action* | Extra Odds increase money at risk and can amplify dollar swings |

*These figures refer to standard bet-resolution calculations, not expected loss per physical dice roll. The effective edge of a combined betting approach depends on how much is wagered in each component. See the full [craps house-edge chart](/strategy/craps/house-edge/).*

## 1. Pass Line: the best place to learn

Make a Pass Line bet before the come-out roll. You win immediately on 7 or 11, lose on 2, 3, or 12, and establish a **point** on 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10. After a point is set, you win if the point rolls again before a 7 and lose if 7 arrives first. The bet pays even money and has an approximately **1.41%** house edge.

**Example:** You put $10 on Pass Line. The come-out is 8. If the shooter rolls another 8 before a 7, you win $10; if a 7 comes first, you lose $10. Other rolls keep the decision going.

**Beginner verdict:** The simplest foundation. Follow the action in the [how-to-play guide](/strategy/craps/how-to-play/) and then [practice free craps](/games/craps/).

## 2. Add Odds only if the extra exposure makes sense

Once a point exists, many tables let you place an Odds bet behind your Pass Line wager. That additional bet pays at the true mathematical odds of the point returning before 7: **2:1 on 4/10, 3:2 on 5/9, and 6:5 on 6/8**. The Odds portion has **zero house edge**.

Say the point is 8, you have $10 on Pass Line, and you add $10 in Odds. If 8 arrives before 7, your flat bet earns $10 and your Odds earn $12. If 7 arrives first, **both bets lose** for a $20 loss.

Zero-edge Odds do not turn the total bet into an advantage, and they do not make the game risk-free. They lower the blended casino edge on the money you stake, but put more money in play. If you're working with a small bankroll, there's no requirement to take maximum Odds. The [Pass Line and Odds guide](/strategy/craps/pass-line-and-odds/) explains limits and payouts in more detail.

## 3. Place 6 and 8: a flexible alternative

Unlike Pass Line, Place bets can be made once the game is underway. For 6 and 8, each bet pays **7:6**, so use multiples of $6 to get a whole-dollar payout. A $12 Place 6 pays **$14 profit** when a 6 arrives before a 7; a 7 before a 6 loses the $12 wager. The same applies to Place 8.

Each number has five dice combinations, versus six for the 7, which gives each Place 6/8 bet an approximately **1.52%** house edge when it resolves. Placing **both** numbers increases how much money is exposed; it does not reduce the underlying edge of either bet.

For a $24 total setup, you might place $12 on 6 and $12 on 8. A hit on either one pays $14 while the other bet remains outstanding, assuming both are working and you leave the winning bet up. A seven-out costs the exposed $24. You can ask for a bet to be taken down or turned off, depending on the table's rules.

## 4. Come bets: add action without chasing proposition bets

A Come bet is basically a new Pass Line bet begun after a point is established. On its first roll it wins on 7/11, loses on 2/3/12, or travels to its own number. Then it wins if that number returns before 7. The flat Come bet has the same approximate **1.41%** house edge as Pass Line.

Come betting is useful if you want more than one live number, but it adds bookkeeping and can result in multiple wagers losing together on a 7. Learn one Come bet first before considering more involved systems such as the [3 Point Molly](/the-pit/3-point-molly-craps-strategy/).

## 5. Don't Pass: low edge, different experience

Don't Pass generally wins when 7 appears before the established point, and it loses if the point repeats first. On the come-out, 2 or 3 wins and 7 or 11 loses; 12 is a push under the rules used here. Its **~1.36%** edge is slightly lower than the Pass Line's, but a fractional edge difference should not drive you to a betting style you don't understand.

## Strategies beginners should treat cautiously

**The Iron Cross** covers many totals and can generate frequent winning rolls, but 7 can wipe out several positions at once. Read the [Iron Cross simulation](/the-pit/iron-cross-simulated/) for the cost of that coverage. **Hardways, Any Seven, and many proposition bets** have materially higher house edges; their appealing payouts conceal lower hit probabilities. A progression such as increasing bets after a loss does not change the expected value of the underlying wager.

## Which approach should you actually try?

If you're learning the layout, make a small Pass Line wager and watch a few points resolve. Once comfortable, experiment with one Odds bet. If you prefer having specific numbers in action, practice one $6 Place 6 or Place 8 wager before putting both out. Above all, compare **total dollars at risk**, not simply the published edge percentage. A lower-edge approach that requires much larger wagers can still cost more in absolute dollars.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the simplest craps strategy for beginners?

A small Pass Line bet, followed by an optional Odds bet once a point is established, is an easy place to begin. Neither wager guarantees a win.

### Is placing 6 and 8 better than Pass Line?

Not strictly. Pass Line has a slightly lower flat-bet house edge, while Place 6/8 offers more flexibility and faster bet decisions. Your choice also affects how many dollars you keep exposed.

### Does taking maximum Odds make craps profitable?

No. Odds pay at fair odds, but your Pass Line wager still has a house edge. More Odds can increase session volatility.

### What is the best craps strategy to win every time?

There isn't one. Fair dice produce unpredictable outcomes, and ordinary casino wagers are priced with a long-run house advantage.
