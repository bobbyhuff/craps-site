**Quick answer:** The 6/8 strategy means placing bets on both 6 and 8, two relatively common dice totals. Each Place bet pays **7:6**, carries about a **1.52% house edge** under standard payouts, and loses if a 7 appears before that individual number. The strategy is simple to understand and flexible to manage, but it **does not beat the casino**.

Craps players often recommend 6 and 8 because each total has five of the 36 equally likely dice combinations. Only 7, with six combinations, appears more often. You can see the bets in action on the [0stakes free craps table](/games/craps/).

## Step by step: how the 6/8 strategy works

1. After the point is established, tell the dealer you'd like to **place the 6 and 8**. On a standard live table they are usually off on the come-out unless you say they're working.
2. Bet an amount divisible by $6 on each number so a 7:6 payout produces whole dollars: for example, **$12 on 6 and $12 on 8**.
3. Each time 6 rolls before 7, your working Place 6 wins **$14 profit** on a $12 stake. Each time 8 rolls before 7, your working Place 8 wins **$14 profit**.
4. Leave the bets up to collect further hits, take one or both down, or adjust them according to the available table rules. If 7 appears while both are working, **both exposed stakes are lost**.

The 6 and 8 bets are two separate wagers. A 6 payout doesn't automatically settle the 8; it remains exposed. Likewise, the 8 can hit multiple times while the 6 doesn't show up.

## Why 6 and 8 are popular: the 36 dice combinations

Two dice create 36 equally likely ordered combinations. There are **five combinations for 6**, five for 8, and **six for 7**. The remaining 20 combinations correspond to other totals.

| Next roll | Combinations | Probability |
|---|---:|---:|
| 6 | 5 | 13.89% |
| 8 | 5 | 13.89% |
| 7 | 6 | 16.67% |
| Anything else | 20 | 55.56% |

When both bets are active, a 6 or 8 appears on **10 of 36 rolls (27.78%)**; a 7 arrives on **6 of 36 (16.67%)**. That is **not** a 62.5% chance of profiting on your very next roll. Instead, *conditional on the first roll that is 6, 8, or 7*, the chance it is 6 or 8 is **10/16 = 62.5%**. A hit produces $14 on one $12 position, whereas a 7 can cost the entire $24 setup. Frequency alone does not determine profitability.

## Example: $12 on 6 and $12 on 8

You begin with $24 on the layout. Imagine rolls of **4, 6, 5, 8, 7**, while both Place bets are working and you keep the original stakes active.

- Roll 4: neither bet resolves, $0 profit or loss.
- Roll 6: collect **$14** profit from Place 6; the $12 stake stays up.
- Roll 5: neither bet resolves.
- Roll 8: collect **$14** profit from Place 8; the $12 stake stays up.
- Roll 7: lose **$24** total in still-exposed stakes.

**Net: +$4** over that specific sequence (**$28** in payouts minus **$24** in lost stakes). Another sequence could seven-out immediately for **−$24**, or produce several more hits first. This is why short winning runs are possible despite a long-run house edge.

## How the 1.52% house edge is calculated

Look at a **single $12 Place 6**. The bet is decided when a 6 or 7 eventually appears; other rolls don't settle it. Of those 11 relevant combinations, 5 win and 6 lose. A win pays $14, while a loss costs $12.

Expected net profit per resolved bet is:

**(5/11 × $14) − (6/11 × $12) = −$2/11 ≈ −$0.1818**.

Divide that by the $12 initial stake: **0.1818 / 12 ≈ 1.515%** expected loss per resolved Place bet. Place 8 has identical mathematics. Two active bets do not magically cancel the edge; they add exposure and can generate correlated losses on a 7.

This expected-loss figure is **per resolved bet**, not per physical dice roll. Your actual session outcomes can swing considerably around that average.

## Should you press your winnings or collect them?

There are two common approaches. **Collect** keeps the original stakes at the same size while banking each $14 payout. **Press** increases the amount at risk after a hit in pursuit of larger future payouts. Pressing changes the volatility and dollar exposure, but does **not** improve the 1.52% house edge of a standard Place 6 or Place 8 bet. You can also use a partial press, but it isn't a way around negative expected value.

If your goal is to practice longer with a fixed pretend bankroll, collecting and keeping stakes modest generally exposes less money than aggressively pressing. That is a bankroll-management choice, not a winning system.

## 6/8 versus Pass Line and Odds

Pass Line has about a **1.41%** house edge; Place 6/8 is slightly higher at **1.52%**. Pass Line can only start on the come-out, while Place 6/8 can be made during a point. A Pass Line Odds bet has **0% house edge**, but is an additional wager that increases the amount at risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do craps players place 6 and 8?

They occur on five dice combinations apiece, more often than any total except 7. Standard Place bets on 6 and 8 also have a relatively low 1.52% house edge.

### What does $12 on 6 and $12 on 8 pay?

Each winning $12 Place bet pays **$14 profit** when its number arrives before 7. Your original stake can remain working if you choose.

### Is a 6/8 strategy better than Pass Line?

Not in house-edge terms: the Pass Line has a slightly lower edge. Place 6/8 offers more flexibility about when to enter and exit a bet.

### Does pressing 6 and 8 make you more likely to win?

No. Pressing increases stakes and potential payouts but does not change the probabilities of the dice or eliminate the house edge.

### Are Place 6 and 8 working on the come-out roll?

At many live tables, Place bets are off on the come-out unless a player requests them to work. Check the rules of the specific game; 0stakes provides a Bets Off control for practicing that difference.
