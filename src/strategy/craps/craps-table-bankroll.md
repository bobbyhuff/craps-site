**Quick answer:** There is no bankroll that guarantees a long craps session. The amount you need depends on the **table minimum, number of simultaneous bets, whether you take odds, and how much you are prepared to lose**. Start by deciding the maximum loss you can comfortably accept, not by choosing a casino's suggested amount.

## First: understand what the minimum actually means

A $15 craps table usually means the Pass Line has a $15 minimum. It does **not** mean you can cover every wager on the layout with $15. A Pass Line bet plus odds requires more chips; Place 6 and 8 adds additional simultaneous exposure.

For example, with a $15 Pass Line bet and 2× odds after a point, you could have **$45 at risk** on that decision. If you also place 6 and 8 for $18 each, you have **$81 committed**. Those bets don't necessarily settle on the same roll, and rules vary between tables.

## A simple planning worksheet

| Plan | Example chips committed after point | Comments |
|---|---:|---|
| $10 Pass Line only | $10 | One decision at a time |
| $15 Pass Line + 2× odds | $45 | Lower blended house edge, larger swings |
| $15 Pass Line + $18 each on 6 and 8 | $51 | Several bets active together |
| $15 Pass Line + 2× odds + $18 on 6 and 8 | $81 | Considerable concurrent exposure |

These are **examples, not recommended bankroll amounts**. Check whether a table allows those bet sizes and odds multiples.

## How to build a sensible session budget

1. Set a loss limit before playing. Only use money you can afford to lose entirely.
2. Check the posted table minimum and allowed odds.
3. Write down your largest possible simultaneous exposure.
4. Decide whether the session is still enjoyable if several decisions lose close together.
5. Use fewer bets or a lower-limit table if your budget feels tight.

A bigger bankroll reduces how quickly a losing run might end a session; it does not change a negative-expectation bet into a winning one.

## What should a beginner actually bet?

For learning, start with a single Pass Line bet and understand the come-out roll and the point. Odds placed behind the Pass Line pay at true odds, but adding them increases the amount exposed. Avoid treating longshot proposition bets as a plan for extending a small bankroll.

## Why you shouldn't use the '20 times the minimum' rule blindly

Rules of thumb fail when they ignore odds, simultaneous bets, and session length. Twenty $15 units means $300, but that can disappear rapidly if you're risking several units at once. Use your full exposure, not just the number printed on the table sign, to plan a budget.

## FAQ

### Is $100 enough for craps?

At some low-minimum tables it might support a short session; at higher-minimum tables or with multiple bets it may not last long at all. There's no universal duration or guarantee.

### Do odds bets improve my chance of leaving ahead?

They reduce the blended house edge per dollar of total action for a Pass Line plus odds combination, but increase wager size and variance. They do not guarantee a winning session.

### Should I chase losses by increasing bets?

No. A previous loss does not improve the probability of the next dice outcome.
