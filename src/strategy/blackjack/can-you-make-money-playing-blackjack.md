**Yes, you can leave a blackjack table with more money than you started with. But winning a session is not the same as having a profitable long-term strategy.** For someone playing ordinary blackjack with basic strategy, the casino usually retains a small mathematical advantage. That means you can win tonight and still expect to lose money, on average, over enough play.

There is a genuine exception: skilled advantage players sometimes find situations where the expected value swings in their favor. That takes more than memorizing a strategy chart, and the advantage is neither guaranteed nor available at every table.

Here's what the math says, without a winning-system sales pitch.

## The short answer: short-term profit is possible; long-term profit takes an edge

Three claims can all be true at once:

- **A single blackjack session can be profitable.** Cards are random, and actual results swing above and below the mathematical average.
- **Basic strategy does not normally eliminate the house edge.** It helps you make the mathematically best hit, stand, double, and split decisions for a given ruleset.
- **Blackjack can sometimes be beaten through legitimate advantage play.** Techniques such as card counting may identify favorable shoe compositions, but require precise execution, bankroll discipline, and suitable conditions.

Don't confuse “I won $150 last night” with “my betting method has a positive expected return.” The first describes an outcome. The second is a mathematical claim requiring evidence.

## What is the house edge in blackjack?

The **house edge** is the casino's theoretical average profit as a share of initial wagers over extensive play. It is not a fee taken from every hand, and it does not predict the outcome of an individual session.

Under favorable rules and accurate basic strategy, blackjack can have a house edge around **0.5%**, but there is no one universal number. Deck count, whether the dealer hits soft 17, surrender availability, doubling and splitting rules, and especially blackjack payouts can change the result.

Imagine a blackjack game with an assumed **0.5% house edge**. The examples below are illustrations of expected loss, not predictions or measured results from the 0stakes simulator.

| Total initial wagers | Expected net result at 0.5% house edge |
|---:|---:|
| $100 | -$0.50 |
| $1,000 | -$5.00 |
| $5,000 | -$25.00 |
| $10,000 | -$50.00 |

**Formula:** theoretical expected loss = total initial wagers × house-edge percentage. Because blackjack includes doubling and splitting, your actual amount at risk and the spread of results may be greater than the starting bet suggests. The formula is a long-run accounting tool, not a statement that a $10,000 session will end exactly $50 down.

A useful takeaway: the edge applies to **money wagered over time**, not simply the bankroll you bring. A $100 starting bankroll can generate much more than $100 in total wagers as winnings are recycled through additional rounds.

## Why do some blackjack players win consistently for a while?

Because short samples are noisy. You might win several sessions in a row, even in a negative-expectation game. Blackjack includes regular wins, losses, ties, natural blackjacks, double-down decisions, and split hands. Those create substantial variance around the average result.

A winning month tells you what happened during that month. Without a credible advantage and a much larger body of results, it does not establish that you've beaten the game mathematically.

The inverse is also true: even an advantage player can have a losing session or a prolonged downswing. **Positive expected value never means guaranteed profit.**

## Does basic strategy make blackjack profitable?

**Not by itself.** Basic strategy chooses the action with the best mathematical expectation for your hand and the dealer's visible card under specified rules. In a conventional casino game, that usually means reducing the casino's edge rather than reversing it.

For example, the correct response to a hard 16 can change with the dealer's upcard. Splitting 8s is normally different from splitting 10s. These aren't matters of intuition: they are calculated decisions that protect your expected return.

## Can card counting really beat blackjack?

**Under the right conditions, it can create a player advantage.** Unlike a roulette wheel, a blackjack shoe changes as cards are dealt. The proportion of high and low cards remaining matters to the odds of future hands. Card-counting methods attempt to estimate that shift and adjust decisions or bet sizes accordingly.

But card counting is not a shortcut to easy income. It requires accuracy under pressure, enough penetration into the shoe before reshuffling, favorable rules, an adequate bankroll to withstand variance, and the ability to increase wagers when conditions justify it. Casinos may also limit or refuse play to counters in many jurisdictions. A counting method's advantage depends on the exact game and how it's executed; no fixed profit rate should be promised.

A continuous-shuffling setup or shallow penetration can substantially undermine the opportunities a counter would need. And simply raising your bets after you lose is **not** card counting: it adds risk without changing the probability of the next hand in your favor.

## The payout rule that can quietly cost you money

Before thinking about any betting system, check whether a natural blackjack pays **3:2** or **6:5**.

On a $10 bet:

| Natural blackjack payout | Net win |
|---|---:|
| 3:2 | $15 |
| 6:5 | $12 |

That $3 difference on the same winning hand adds up. All else equal, switching from 3:2 to 6:5 increases the house edge by approximately **1.4 percentage points**. In a game with a hypothetical 0.5% house edge at 3:2, a comparable 6:5 version could be near 1.9%, with the exact total depending on other rules.

## Do betting systems like Martingale work in blackjack?

Systems that increase the wager after a loss or follow a fixed pattern can change how wins and losses are distributed. They do **not**, by themselves, turn a negative expected-value game into a positive one.

Suppose you double your base bet after every loss. A few wins may make the approach look reliable, but one long bad run can require a much larger next wager than your bankroll or a table limit permits. Blackjack also includes pushes and outcomes involving doubles and splits, making simplified 'double after every loss' stories especially misleading.

Bet sizing controls **how much you risk**. Unless it responds to real information that changes the odds, it doesn't create an advantage. For practical limits and risk, see [0stakes' bankroll management guide](/strategy/bankroll-management/).

## How much can you realistically expect to win or lose?

You cannot estimate a reliable session profit from just your starting bankroll. You need the rules, strategy, starting bet, number of rounds, and how doubles and splits are handled. Even then, the expected value is an average across many possible outcomes, not a forecast of what will happen to you.

Consider a **hypothetical** player who makes 100 initial wagers of $10 in a game with a 0.5% edge. The initial wagering volume is $1,000 and the theoretical expected loss is about $5. The actual outcome might be well above or below that figure. A player could even go broke before completing 100 rounds. The estimate does not account for an adaptive bet plan, and should not be mistaken for the result of a fresh simulation.

## What actually helps you play a better blackjack game?

If you're playing for entertainment rather than pursuing advanced advantage play, the most useful improvements are straightforward:

1. **Choose better rules.** Prefer 3:2 payouts; know whether the dealer stands or hits soft 17, and whether you may double after a split.
2. **Use a rules-matched basic strategy chart.** Avoid improvising on tough hands.
3. **Skip insurance unless you have a mathematically justified advantage.** It is generally a poor side bet for a non-counter. See [0stakes' insurance analysis](/the-pit/blackjack-insurance-simulated/).
4. **Set a fixed entertainment budget.** Don't chase losses or treat a win target as protection against the house edge.
5. **Practice without risking money.** Use the [free blackjack table](/games/blackjack/) to learn decision-making and the [strategy trainer](/strategy/blackjack/) to locate the training resource.

None of these steps guarantees a profitable session. They make the experience more informed and can reduce avoidable mistakes.

## FAQ

### Is blackjack a game of skill or luck?

Both. The cards you receive are random, but the decisions you make affect expected outcomes. Skill matters more in blackjack than in games without meaningful player decisions, but ordinary basic strategy usually does not overcome the casino's built-in edge.

### Can you make a living playing blackjack?

A small number of experienced advantage players have pursued blackjack professionally. That is very different from reliably earning a paycheck by playing ordinary basic strategy. Advantage play has significant variance, requires capital and expertise, and faces practical casino restrictions.

### What percentage of blackjack players win money?

There is no single reliable percentage that applies to all players or sessions. It depends on the rules, skill, bet sizes, length of play, and even how 'win' is defined. A session win rate is not the same thing as a long-run expected return.

### Can you win blackjack without counting cards?

Yes, you can win individual hands and sessions without counting. That does not make your long-term expectation positive. Favorable promotions or unusual game conditions can sometimes matter, but they need to be evaluated carefully and do not guarantee a profit.

### Does playing longer improve your chances of making money?

Not in a negative-expectation game. More wagering generally means a larger expected dollar loss, even though your actual results can continue to fluctuate above or below that expectation.

## Bottom line

**You can make money playing blackjack in the short run. Most recreational players, however, do not have a long-run mathematical advantage.** Basic strategy keeps mistakes down; good table rules keep the cost of playing lower; and genuine advantage play is a specialized discipline, not a guaranteed system.
