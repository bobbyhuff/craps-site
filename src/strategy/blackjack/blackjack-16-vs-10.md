**Quick answer: Usually yes.** On the 0stakes six-deck blackjack game, basic strategy says **hit a hard 16 against a dealer 10**, except when you have a pair of 8s: **split the 8s**. If a casino offers late surrender, a two-card hard 16 against 10 is generally better surrendered. The 0stakes game does not list surrender among its available actions.

## Why a 16 feels impossible

If you hit hard 16, any 6, 7, 8, 9, or ten-value card busts you immediately. Standing looks tempting, but the dealer's 10 upcard is powerful and a total of 16 loses to every dealer 17–21. The correct choice is the **less unfavorable** play, not a safe play.

## Decision table

| Your hand | Dealer shows 10 | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| Hard 10 + 6 | 10 | Hit; surrender first if permitted |
| Hard 9 + 7 | 10 | Hit; surrender first if permitted |
| Pair of 8s | 10 | Split under standard six-deck S17 basic strategy |
| Soft 16 (Ace + 5) | 10 | Hit |
| Multi-card hard 16 | 10 | Standard total-dependent chart says hit; composition-dependent exceptions may favor standing |

This table follows a total-dependent basic-strategy baseline. **Composition-dependent** play may differ slightly once you know the exact cards already seen, especially in close decisions like 16 versus 10.

## Does hitting mean you're likely to win?

No. This is often a negative-expected-value hand whichever way you play it. The purpose of basic strategy is to select the move with the least expected loss given the known information and table rules.

## What about surrender?

Late surrender lets you give up a hand in exchange for losing half the original bet, generally after the dealer checks for blackjack. It is not offered at every table. Where it is available, standard charts often call for surrendering a two-card hard 16 versus a 10. Never assume a real casino has the same rules as the practice game.

## Why a pair of 8s is different

Two 8s total 16, but splitting creates two separate hands starting at 8. Depending on rules, you commit an additional wager and each new hand can improve. That's why the pair chart takes precedence over the hard-total chart.

## FAQ

### Should you always hit 16?

No. Against weak dealer upcards such as 2–6, basic strategy typically stands on hard 16. Pairs and surrender rules also matter.

### What if the dealer has an ace instead of a 10?

The correct action still depends on hand composition and table rules. Consult the specific chart, rather than applying one blanket rule.

### Is 16 versus 10 a close decision?

Yes. That's partly why advanced composition-dependent analyses can differ from a simplified strategy chart.
