**Quick answer:** In a six-deck game where the dealer stands on soft 17, surrender a **hard 16 against a dealer 9, 10 or Ace**, and a **hard 15 against a dealer 10**. Play every other hand. A pair of 8s is not a surrender hand: split it.

Surrender means giving up the hand before you act and getting half your bet back. It is the only move in blackjack that locks in a loss on purpose, and it is correct only when playing the hand loses even more.

## Late surrender chart (6 decks, dealer stands on soft 17)

| Your hand | Dealer 9 | Dealer 10 | Dealer Ace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard 16 (10-6, 9-7) | Surrender | Surrender | Surrender |
| Hard 15 (10-5, 9-6) | Hit | Surrender | Hit |
| Pair of 8s | Split | Split | Split |
| Hard 14 or less | Hit | Hit | Hit |
| Hard 17 | Stand | Stand | Stand |

If the dealer hits soft 17, the list grows: a hard 15 and a hard 17 against an Ace also become surrenders, and many charts add 8-8 against an Ace.

Surrender is only offered on your first two cards. Once you hit, the option is gone, so a three-card 16 is played the normal way.

## The 50% line

Surrender costs exactly half a bet, every time. So the rule is simple: surrender when playing the hand is expected to lose more than half a bet.

Put another way, ignoring pushes, a hand has to win less than one time in four before surrender beats playing it. Win 25% and lose 75%, and you lose a net 50 cents per dollar, the same as surrendering.

Very few hands are that bad. These are the ones that cross the line:

| Hand | Best play without surrender | Approx. loss per $1 bet | Surrender loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard 16 vs 10 | Hit | about 54 cents | 50 cents |
| Hard 16 vs Ace | Hit | about 52 cents | 50 cents |
| Hard 16 vs 9 | Hit | about 51 cents | 50 cents |
| Hard 15 vs 10 | Hit | about 50.4 cents | 50 cents |

The gains are small on each hand. A hard 16 against a 10 saves about 4 cents per dollar bet. That is why surrender is a minor rule, not a strategy on its own.

## What the rule is worth

Late surrender lowers the house edge by about 0.07% to 0.08% in a six-deck game when it is used correctly. For comparison, a 6:5 blackjack payout instead of {{ blackjackOdds.blackjackPayoutStr }} adds roughly 1.4% to the house edge. Check the blackjack payout first, then worry about surrender.

Used badly, surrender costs money. Giving up a hard 12 against a 10, or a 16 against a 7, throws away hands that lose less than half a bet when played. The chart above is the complete list. Anything not on it should be played.

## Late surrender vs early surrender

**Late surrender** is the common version. The dealer checks for blackjack first. If the dealer has it, you lose the whole bet and surrender never comes up.

**Early surrender** lets you give up before the dealer checks, so you can escape a dealer blackjack for half a bet. It is worth roughly 0.6% to the player, which is why it is almost never offered.

## How to surrender at a real table

Say "surrender" before you take any other action. The hand signal is a horizontal line drawn on the felt behind your bet with one finger. The dealer takes half your bet and your cards.

Tables rarely post the rule. If you don't see it on the felt or the placard, ask the dealer whether surrender is offered before you need it.

## The takeaway

Surrender four hands and no others: hard 16 against 9, 10 and Ace, and hard 15 against 10. It saves a few cents per dollar on the worst spots in the game.
