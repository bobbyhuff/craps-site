A casino doesn't literally win every night, every table, or every session. **It wins on average over repeated play because the payout is slightly less than the mathematical fair price of most bets.** That difference is the house edge.

## House edge in plain English

Suppose a bet pays $1 profit when it wins and loses $1 when it loses. On American roulette, a bet on red wins in 18 of 38 pockets and loses in 20. Its expected result is `(18/38 × $1) − (20/38 × $1) = −$0.05263` per $1 wagered. The house edge is therefore about **5.26%**.

That doesn't mean you lose exactly 5.26% of your bankroll each night. It means the average expected loss is 5.26% **of money wagered** across that kind of bet.

## The law of large numbers

Over many independent trials, the proportion of outcomes tends to converge toward its theoretical probability. That doesn't force a short losing or winning streak to reverse itself. It simply makes the long-run average more stable as the number of trials grows.

A gambler might win $300 tonight. Another could lose $500. The casino can still have a mathematical advantage when thousands of wagers across many customers are considered.

## Three different concepts

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| House edge | Average loss as percentage of wagered amount | About 5.26% for an American roulette red bet |
| Variance | How much results fluctuate around the average | A single roulette session can finish well ahead |
| Total action | Sum of all wagers made | 100 × $10 bets = $1,000 wagered |

House edge times total action gives **expected loss**, not a guaranteed actual loss.

## Different games, different edges

On conventional rules, a standard baccarat Banker bet has about a **1.06%** house edge after commission. A craps Pass Line bet has about **1.41%** house edge. American roulette's standard wagers are usually about **5.26%**. Blackjack depends on rules and player decisions; don't treat it as having a single universal percentage.

## Why betting systems don't fix the edge

Doubling after losses, betting the opposite color, or quitting after a win changes wager amounts and session shape. It doesn't change roulette's underlying 18/38 probability of red. When the expected return per wager is negative, making more wagers generally adds more expected loss.

## Can the player ever have an edge?

Occasionally, yes, in circumstances outside ordinary fixed-odds recreational play: skilled card counting under suitable blackjack conditions, some promotions, or certain genuinely advantageous pricing situations. That doesn't negate the house edge on standard wagers, and advantage play comes with practical limits and risk.

## FAQ

### If a casino has an edge, how do people win jackpots?

Because short-run outcomes are random. A rare large payout is fully compatible with a negative average return.

### Can a casino lose money on a particular day?

Yes. The mathematical advantage is an average over many bets, not a promise of daily profits.

### Does betting less remove the house edge?

It lowers the dollars exposed, not the percentage advantage embedded in the bet.
