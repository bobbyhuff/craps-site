**Quick answer:** The Pai Gow Poker joker is only **semi-wild**. In your five-card hand it can complete a straight, a flush or a straight flush. If it can't do any of those, it's an **Ace**. In your two-card hand it's **always an Ace**. It can never make a pair, two pair, trips, a full house or four of a kind out of anything but Aces.

These are the rules used by the [0stakes Pai Gow Poker game](/games/pai-gow-poker/) and by most American casinos. Players often call this joker "the bug".

## Why Pai Gow Poker uses a joker

The deck is 52 cards plus one joker ({{ paiGowOdds.deckSize }} total). The joker adds a little more action: it makes straights and flushes more common in a game where you need two hands good enough to beat the dealer. Keeping it semi-wild stops it from turning ordinary hands into monsters, which would change the game's balance far more.

## Rule 1: the joker as an Ace

By default the joker is an Ace. That means:

| Five-card hand | What it counts as |
|---|---|
| Joker K♣ K♦ 7♥ 2♠ | Pair of Kings, **Ace** kicker |
| Joker A♣ A♦ 7♥ 2♠ | **Three Aces** |
| Joker K♣ K♦ Q♥ Q♠ | Two pair, Kings and Queens, **Ace** kicker |
| Joker K♣ K♦ K♠ 2♠ | Three Kings, **Ace** kicker (not four Kings) |
| Joker A♣ A♦ A♠ 2♠ | **Four Aces** |
| Joker A♣ A♦ A♠ A♥ | **Five Aces**, the best hand in the game |

The joker can pair up with real Aces. It can't pair up with anything else.

## Rule 2: completing a straight

If the joker can fill a gap or an end of a straight, it does, and it takes whatever rank makes the **highest** straight.

| Five cards | Result |
|---|---|
| Joker 9♣ 10♦ J♥ Q♠ | King-high straight (Joker = K) |
| Joker A♥ K♦ Q♣ J♠ | Ace-high straight (Joker = 10) |
| Joker 3♥ 4♦ 5♣ 6♠ | Seven-high straight (Joker = 7) |
| Joker 2♥ 3♦ 4♣ 5♠ | A-2-3-4-5 (Joker = Ace) |

The last row is a nice detail: with 2-3-4-5, the joker could make a 6-high straight or the wheel. Because the wheel (A-2-3-4-5) is the **second-highest straight** in this ruleset, the joker becomes an Ace and makes the stronger hand.

## Rule 3: completing a flush

With four cards of one suit, the joker completes the flush. It counts as the **highest card of that suit you don't already hold**:

| Five cards | Result |
|---|---|
| Joker 2♥ 7♥ 9♥ K♥ | Flush, A-K-9-7-2 (Joker = A♥) |
| Joker A♥ 7♥ 9♥ K♥ | Flush, A-K-Q-9-7 (Joker = Q♥) |

That second case trips people up. You already have the A♥, so the joker can't be another one. It becomes the next highest missing heart, the Queen.

## Rule 4: completing a straight flush

Four cards to a straight flush plus the joker makes a straight flush, again choosing the highest possible:

| Five cards | Result |
|---|---|
| Joker 5♠ 6♠ 7♠ 8♠ | Nine-high straight flush |
| Joker 9♥ J♥ Q♥ K♥ | King-high straight flush (Joker = 10♥) |
| Joker 10♠ J♠ Q♠ K♠ | **Royal Flush** (Joker = A♠) |

## Rule 5: the joker in the two-card hand

In the two-card hand the joker is **always an Ace**. There are no straights or flushes in a two-card hand, so there's nothing else it could do.

| Two-card hand | Result |
|---|---|
| Joker K♦ | A-K |
| Joker 7♣ | A-7 |
| Joker A♠ | Pair of Aces, the best possible low hand |

Joker plus a King in the two-card hand is the strongest non-pair low hand you can have, which makes the joker valuable even when it doesn't complete anything.

## Legal and illegal interpretations

| Claim | Legal? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Joker + 9-9 + 5-2 = three 9s | **No** | The joker only pairs with Aces. It's a pair of 9s with an Ace kicker. |
| Joker + K-K-Q-Q = full house | **No** | Not a straight or flush. Two pair, Ace kicker. |
| Joker + K-K-K = four Kings | **No** | Three Kings, Ace kicker. |
| Joker + A-A-A = four Aces | **Yes** | The joker is an Ace. |
| Joker + four hearts = flush | **Yes** | Completes a flush. |
| Joker + 3-4-5-6 = straight | **Yes** | Completes a straight, as a 7. |
| Joker + K = pair of Kings (low hand) | **No** | Two-card hand: always an Ace. A-K. |

## How the joker changes hand setting

Because the joker is an Ace unless it completes something, it often belongs in the two-card hand when your five-card hand doesn't need it. Two examples from the 0stakes house way:

- **Joker A♥ 7♥ 4♥ 2♥ K♦ Q♣:** the joker completes an Ace-high heart flush, so the flush goes high and K-Q goes low.
- **Joker 8♦ 9♣ 10♠ J♥ 3♦ 3♣:** the joker completes a Queen-high straight, which frees the pair of 3s to go low.

Without a straight or flush to complete, a lone joker usually acts as the Ace it is: kept high if it's your top card, or played low as A-x when that makes the better two-card hand. The full rules are in the [house way guide](/strategy/pai-gow-poker/house-way/).

## Common misconceptions

- **"The joker is wild."** Only for straights and flushes. Everywhere else it's an Ace.
- **"The joker can be any card in a flush."** It's the highest card of that suit you don't hold.
- **"Joker plus four of a kind is five of a kind."** Only with four Aces. Four Kings plus the joker is four Kings with an Ace kicker.
- **"The joker can make a two-card straight."** Two-card hands don't have straights.
- **"House rules never vary."** A few casinos play the joker differently, especially in side bets. Ask before you play elsewhere.

## FAQ

### Is the joker wild in Pai Gow Poker?

Partly. It completes straights, flushes and straight flushes. Otherwise it counts as an Ace.

### Can the joker make five of a kind?

Only five Aces: four Aces plus the joker. It's the highest hand in the game.

### What is the joker in the two-card hand?

Always an Ace.

### Can the joker complete a straight flush?

Yes, including a Royal Flush.

### What does the joker count as in a flush if I already have the Ace?

The highest card of that suit you don't already hold, such as the King or Queen.
