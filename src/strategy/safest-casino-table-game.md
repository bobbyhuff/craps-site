**There isn't one universal 'safest' game.** If you mean **lowest house edge under standard rules**, baccarat Banker and low-edge craps bets are strong contenders; well-played 3:2 blackjack can have a low house edge too. But if you mean **the smallest chance of losing an entire session bankroll**, wager size, number of rounds, and volatility matter just as much.

## Compare the math, not the atmosphere

| Game or bet | Typical house edge | Important condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Craps odds bet | 0% on the odds portion | Must accompany an eligible line/come bet; those have their own edge |
| Baccarat Banker | ~1.06% | Standard 5% commission |
| Craps Pass Line | ~1.41% | Standard payout rules |
| Baccarat Player | ~1.24% | Standard rules |
| European roulette standard bets | ~2.70% | Single-zero wheel, absent special rule |
| American roulette standard bets | ~5.26% | Double-zero wheel |
| Blackjack | Rules- and strategy-dependent | Basic strategy, payout, deck count, and dealer rules all matter |

This table compares **house edge per dollar wagered**, not the chance of leaving ahead. A zero-edge odds bet is not a standalone casino game and shouldn't be presented as a free-money strategy.

## Low house edge does not mean low volatility

A $10 Banker wager and a $100 Banker wager have the same percentage house edge but very different dollar exposure. Roulette straight-up bets win infrequently and pay more, while even-money bets hit more often; both generally retain the same percentage edge on the same wheel.

## What about blackjack?

Blackjack is unusual because player decisions matter. Basic strategy reduces mistakes, but the precise edge depends on whether a blackjack pays 3:2 or 6:5, whether the dealer hits soft 17, the number of decks, and other rules. Never rank it with a universal fixed edge without specifying a ruleset.

## What about playing less?

If minimizing expected loss is your goal, reducing total money wagered and the number of rounds has a direct effect. Choosing a lower-edge wager helps **per dollar of action** but doesn't excuse wagering far more than you planned.

## Example: compare the cost of $1,000 in total bets

- Standard baccarat Banker at 1.06%: expected loss of about **$10.60**.
- Craps Pass Line at 1.41%: expected loss of about **$14.10**.
- American roulette at 5.26%: expected loss of about **$52.60**.

These are statistical expectations, not predicted results of any one session.

## Which game should a beginner learn first?

Baccarat has straightforward decisions because the cards are drawn automatically. Roulette is visually simple. Craps has many bets and a learning curve. Blackjack needs the most attention to playing decisions. For a new player, clarity and enjoyment can matter more than choosing between small differences in house edge on fictional-money games.

## FAQ

### Is baccarat better than roulette mathematically?

Under the stated standard rules, Banker has a lower house edge than standard American or European roulette bets.

### Is craps a safe bet?

No wager with a positive house edge is guaranteed to win; odds bets are fair-priced but attached to another bet.

### Does a lower house edge guarantee I keep my bankroll longer?

No. The number and size of bets, volatility, and luck determine individual outcomes.
