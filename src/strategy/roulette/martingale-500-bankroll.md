It depends on the first bet. **With a $5 opening bet and a $500 bankroll, you can fund six consecutive Martingale bets** (through $160), committing **$315**, and you cannot place the next full $320 bet after six losses with the $185 remaining.

## The doubling formula

For starting bet **b**, total cost of **n** consecutive losing bets is:

`b × (2^n − 1)`

To fund the complete next bet, the total amount needed must be no greater than your bankroll. The formula assumes no table maximum, no other bets, and that each bet loses.

## Starting bet matters

| Opening bet | Consecutive bets $500 funds | Total after those losses | Next required bet |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $1 | 8 | $255 | $256 |
| $5 | 6 | $315 | $320 |
| $10 | 5 | $310 | $320 |
| $25 | 4 | $375 | $400 |
| $50 | 3 | $350 | $400 |

At $1, the ninth bet would be $256, but only $245 remains. This is why the last column should always be compared with cash remaining, not with the original $500.

## Why the system feels like it works

A win at any step of a completed even-money Martingale cycle recovers the previous losses and earns exactly the original base bet in net profit. But each additional loss doubles the required wager, creating a very uneven risk-to-reward pattern.

## American roulette risk

A red bet loses on 20 of 38 pockets. For a specified run of six losses, the probability is `(20/38)^6`, approximately **2.13%**. Repeating many cycles makes encountering such a run more likely. A real table maximum may stop doubling even earlier.

## Does switching to European roulette fix it?

No. Single-zero roulette reduces the house edge, but no doubling progression removes it. The system changes cash requirements and the shape of session results, not the expected profitability of a bet.

## FAQ

### Can $500 always survive six consecutive losses?

Not for every starting bet. A $5 base funds six losing wagers; a $10 base funds only five.

### What if the table limit is $100?

The progression stops sooner if the next required wager exceeds the table's limit.

### Can I guarantee $5 profit by doubling?

No. Losing streaks and financial/table limits prevent guaranteed recovery.
